# Connected sessions

[Architecture index](overview.md) · [Canonical instructions](../../AGENTS.md)

Every connection's complete connector start (spawn, handshake and first thread or ready state) has the shared 90-second `START_MS` budget in `sessions/manager.ts`: expiry ends the generation as failed with a fixed connection-named error, stops its owned groups through `sessions/process.ts`'s `ProcessScope` and `core/children.ts`'s identity checks, refuses late spawns and events, rejects waiting opens and restarts, and lets a later open start afresh.

## Prompt protocol

`src/shared/model/prompts.ts` holds B13's discriminated approval, question, plan and form schemas, the private-key raise/end events, the core's prompt and nested Needs-preview types, and the adapter-answer and outer reply shapes. API approvals and plans require an offered rejection; terminal-only observations may carry no choices or questions. Choice effects preserve the distinction between absent (unknown) and an empty object (no additional effect). Answer validation checks offered values, declared feedback, exact question ids, distinct labels and exclusive pick-or-text answers. It performs no authority classification. An adapter may mark a raise `omitted` (it left out a decision its CLI offers that it can't word completely); like `signals`, that never leaves the core. A form may name the MCP server that asks (`server`, the name it is configured under: its words, untrusted), which the core's title for it uses.

**The session seam (B13 C3).** A session raises and ends prompts through its host (`SessionHost.emit` takes `HostEvent`: its session events, `prompt` and `prompt-ended`), each by a key the adapter mints fresh (`randomUUID()`) for every native request or observation and maps privately to its native ids: never reused, parsed, or put in argv or a path. The core answers through the session's `LiveSession.prompts` (`PromptAnswers`): `answer(key, answer)` hands the answer to the native request synchronously and throws, changing nothing, for a definitive refusal; `decline(key, why)` answers with the connection's nonapproval and ends the prompt, never throwing. Every request still pending as a session closes gets that nonapproval from the session itself. A session whose prompts are answered in its terminal also takes `dismiss(key)` (B13 C7's Not waiting): you said a wait it raised from its CLI's report alone isn't one, so it stops raising that observation until a distinct wait, telling its CLI nothing. The Agent SDK's adapter (B13 C4: [Claude Code](claude-code.md)) and Codex's App Server adapter (B13 C5, its own asks too: implementing a plan, consent to use a ChatGPT plan: [Codex](codex.md)) raise through this seam, and so do both Terminal connections (B13 C6: what their CLIs wait on in their terminals, below), whose `PromptAnswers` answer and decline nothing.

**Until X10 converts the renderer (B13 C4; X11 removes this).** The window's cards, Needs' items for prompts, delivery's "nothing asked" check, the silent-turn watchdog and the agents' counts still read the events B13 replaces, so a converted adapter shows each prompt it raises as one of them too, unless the owner declined it as it was raised, and ends it there when the CLI stops waiting, the owner declines it or the session closes. Nothing answers its CLI but the owner: its `LiveSession.answer` refuses, and the `Bridge` it installs through its host as its start begins (`SessionHost.bridge`, before it raises anything) translates an answer to the old event into the prompt's own and names the prompt's key, answering nothing. The manager's `bridge` adds the generation (none where the session shows no such twin, or none runs or starts; it never starts one), and marks such a session's raises `mirrored` in their `PromptOrigin`, from the first: one raised before its start resolves (an MCP server's form as its CLI starts) is shown once too, its old event standing for it, and its answer refused as still starting (B13 C3) until the session runs. The owner lists no mirrored prompt in Needs (the old event's item stands for it) and finds one by its key (`keyed`). The reply router's prompt handler answers an old event's item through the owner: translated, then answered by the owner's NeedId as any prompt is (checked again, reserved, recorded in the owner's words, handed to the session that raised it), then the old event shown answered; a later reply to the old item is told who settled it.

**Terminal observations (B13 C6).** The terminal toolkit's session (`src/main/sessions/terminal/session.ts`) raises what its CLI waits on in its terminal, each `answerIn: 'terminal'` with no choice of corrallm's, once, under a key minted for that observation: what a hook described, as its TerminalCli makes it (a `TerminalSignal` `ask` carries the prompt, in the hook's words: [Claude Code](claude-code.md)); a wait only its CLI's status report describes, as a title-only approval (a question where the report says it asks one) in the report's words, else its last notification's, else the CLI's own (`words.waiting`), with what its screen shows of it where its TerminalCli reads that (`waitDetail`, read once the screen has settled after the wait began: [Codex](codex.md)'s last rows); a notification that it needs the user, a title-only approval; and its folder trust screen, an approval whose subject is `trust` (so yours alone). A redraw or a repeated report is no event; a better description of the same wait (a hook telling it late, a notification's words where the report had none) ends the old one, withdrawn, and raises the new; a report that says something else is another wait. A wait that clears as the CLI goes on working, or gets past its trust screen, ends `answered-natively` (corrallm never says what was chosen); the turn ending (an answer that ends it can't be told from Esc), the CLI exiting or the session closing ends it `withdrawn`. Without status reports, what its hooks told waits until the turn ends, withdrawn then. A wait raised from a report or notification alone that you said isn't one (`dismiss`) isn't raised again while it lasts: a new report, the CLI going on and waiting again, or a hook's description is a distinct wait; a hook-described one, the trust screen, and what its CLI and `asking` show are left as they are, so its session's own readiness still holds a message for it. Until X10, `asking` still lists each, whose Needs items stand for them: the session installs a `Bridge` as its launch begins (its translation refuses: their answers belong to its terminal), so the owner lists none of its raises; delivery, the silent-turn watchdog and the agents' state still read `asking`.

The manager never sends a prompt event to a window: it hands each to its `PromptSink` (the core's prompt owner, `src/main/core/prompts/owner.ts`) with a `PromptOrigin`: the canvas, agent and connection, the generation that raised it, whether that generation is still the agent's and heard, the mode it reported in its first `started` (captured once: its effective start mode), and its answers, bound to that generation as the event came, never looked up again by canvas and agent (a restart's replacement). Before the generation's start has resolved, an answer is refused (its session is still starting) and a decline waits for the session. A raise is CLI activity for the silent-turn watchdog; with no sink composed, it is declined at once. The sink hears every generation's end: as its close begins (a close, a replacement, a canvas leaving its window, quit), when it stops by itself, and when its start fails.

The owner keeps each canvas's live index. A raise is checked whole against the protocol and C6's bounds, a form's link against `link.open`'s policy (`linkTarget`: https, or http to this machine, within MAX_LINK_LENGTH), its key against the live keys of its generation (a duplicate withdraws the prompt that key named and is declined too), and against the budgets (MAX_SESSION_PROMPTS a generation, MAX_CANVAS_PROMPTS a canvas, MAX_CORE_PROMPT_BYTES of the core's), else it is declined and the agent's transcript is told why, in the core's words. A raise from a generation no longer heard is declined without a notice. A terminal observation is never declined: one past a bound waits as a cut stand-in in the core's words (look at the terminal); one past a budget isn't kept, and the transcript says so. An admitted prompt is classified once (`src/main/core/prompts/classify.ts`, B13 C4: a trust or consent subject, any secret question, every form and any native signal make it yours alone; an always, unknown effects on anything but a plain rejection, a rules effect, a network effect beyond the turn, a directories effect, a mode that never asks, ranks above the start mode, isn't listed, or any mode while the start mode is unknown make a choice yours), gets a fresh PromptId and the NeedId `prompt/<escaped agent>/<generation>/<PromptId>`, and never changes after. A terminal's wait raised from its CLI's report alone (no subject, choice, question or plan, and not the core's own stand-in for one it couldn't show) is offered the core's Not waiting, yours alone ([Messages](messages.md)). An end by its key (`withdrawn`, `answered-natively`) or its generation's end ends it; an end for a key unknown, settled or of another generation is ignored, so a late end never ends a replacement. How it is answered is in [Messages](messages.md).

**What each reader sees of a prompt (B13 C7, C11).** One selector, `src/shared/model/prompt-views.ts`, says what you and a token read of a prompt, for every read boundary (Needs and `status`, transcripts and their topic, the counts, the router's refusals, notices, `wait`): you, everything, with each choice as Needs offers it; a token that reaches the agent (or a parent, its team's prompts, through Needs alone), no choice, no form's schema, link, summary or reason (its title is the core's words for everyone in Needs), no detail of a terminal's wait its report alone describes (what the terminal's screen shows, Codex's last rows among it), a hook's tool and input as an SDK prompt's, every question whole and never an answer; a live outcome, yours whole, a token's its type and who settled it. The owner reads it from each live occurrence it lists (`occurrences`: not one its session mirrors until X10), with whether it is the core's stand-in, so a wait its report alone describes (`undescribed`) offers Not waiting wherever it is shown: its Needs items for each reader (`items`), how many wait on an agent (`count`, which `agents` and the summaries add), and the whole prompt as its card would show it (`promptFor`), which the agent's transcript and session topic don't carry yet (where in the transcript it goes, how long one that ended stays with its live outcome, and its topic's publication are B13 C9's). The same selector gives a token the events B13 replaces without an answer, feedback or an "always" label (`entryFor`), and any other item without its choices (`needFor`).

A native user turn synthesized from a prompt answer retains internal `promptReply` provenance through history restoration and merging. The same selector keeps its text yours alone at transcript and session-topic boundaries, replacing it with core wording for tokens; the provenance marker never appears in a wire view.

The shared flat-form reader in `src/shared/model/prompt-forms.ts`, re-exported by prompts.ts, is lifted from the SDK reader, which the Agent SDK's adapter now uses in its place. It supports primitive properties, single and multiple choices, required fields, defaults and the fixed email/URI/date/date-time checks. It re-serializes the parsed subset with no additional properties, drops annotations such as pattern without executing them, and refuses unsupported structure, duplicate values, contradictory bounds and invalid defaults. Accept checks values against that schema; defaults only prefill drafts. Missing required fields refuse; explicit required empty strings or arrays are checked against their actual schema. Optional blank text is omitted as in the existing checker; explicit empty choice values and selections stay values. Decline, cancel and URL forms carry no values.

`src/shared/model/prompt-data.ts` bounds JSON by serialized bytes and depth, rejecting cycles and non-JSON data, and preserves every bounded record key without assigning prototype properties. C6's constants live in `src/shared/api/limits.ts`: a normalized prompt is at most 256 KiB, choices/questions/options at most 16 each, and the core's prompt owner enforces the per-generation, canvas and core budgets. The existing session text/id/form constants re-export their unchanged values from limits.ts. Legacy native question handling retains its old bound until the adapter conversion; the prepared protocol uses MAX_PROMPT_QUESTIONS.

## Product and connections

corrallm is a cross-platform Electron desktop app (Electron Forge + Vite + React + TypeScript). It will become an agent orchestrator that spawns and manages the Claude Code and Codex CLIs as child processes. Today it has a canvas of agents in the renderer (`src/renderer/graph/`), each opening into a Claude Code-style session (`src/renderer/agent/`); canvases saved as `.corral` files through core operations, with a project folder their agents work in. A Claude agent runs the real `claude` CLI, in a Terminal or through the Agent SDK (see Connected sessions): a Terminal agent runs Claude Code's own interactive CLI in a node-pty pseudo-terminal, shown in its agent view with xterm.js (`src/main/sessions/claude-code/`, on the toolkit in `sessions/terminal/`); an Agent SDK agent runs a Claude Code session through the Agent SDK, shown in corrallm's own transcript and composer (`src/main/sessions/claude-code/sdk/`). A Codex agent runs the real `codex` CLI (`src/main/sessions/codex/`, see Codex): in a Terminal, Codex's own interactive CLI on the same terminal plumbing (`src/main/sessions/codex/terminal/`), or through its app server, shown in corrallm's transcript (`src/main/sessions/codex/app-server/`).

## `src/main/sessions/`

Connected sessions (see Connected sessions): adapter.ts is the registry's seam: each adapter's descriptor, executable lookup, connectors, settings reader, fake, conversations, native self-tests, inherited environment keys and usage readers. adapters.ts is the registry (ADAPTERS, the only production file that imports adapter folders) and builds the catalog (see The catalog). connector.ts is the interface a connection implements; manager.ts runs every session for core operations and the remaining session-panel channels (and supplies Terminal screens for the terminal operations); connectors.ts builds connections and settings readers from the registry; installed.ts checks its executable lookups for catalog.installed. locate.ts is that lookup, for connectors, readers and catalog.installed alike: per adapter and agents' environment it shares a search in progress and keeps an executable it found only while it can still run; a miss or a failed search is never kept, so a CLI installed, or moved, after a start said it was missing is found by the next try, without restarting corrallm. fake.ts is the fake for tests, args.ts validates channels' arguments, environment.ts resolves the agents' environment (the login shell's), executables.ts finds CLIs, and transcript.ts words what connections show the same way (paths, output, diffs, usage limits and resets, notices). process.ts is the spawn/execFile gateway: core work records children through core/children.ts in sessions/pids.json, in private groups on POSIX.

Startup verifies their pid/start time/group before ending stale groups, giving SIGTERM up to three seconds before whole-group SIGKILL; cleanup failures warn and keep records for retry.

Windows group cleanup is deferred.

The core supplies SessionManager one directory resolver (`src/main/core/workspaces/resolver.ts`). Every new launch, including restart and the launch after Fresh, resolves that agent's cwd through it; conversation listing, resume's listed-folder check and conversation rewind resolve it too. The legacy implementation returns the picked project TrustedPath, workspace null, generation 0 and no branch; no-project keeps the existing refusal. Directory resolution is part of the existing pending launch: concurrent starts still share it, and closing waits for it before ending the session. Fresh resolves its namespace and readiness before ending the old session. Conversation memory uses the active workspace, or PROJECT_WORKSPACE for non-repositories; a live session pins that namespace, including for late native reports during a pending move. `session:options` accepts an optional agent: with one it uses its verified ready checkout; without one it reads base/user defaults, falling back to the home folder when none was picked. Contextual options are re-read on each use, so a Switch cannot reuse another workspace's options.

Each session keeps the binding it launched in: the resolver's cwd, workspace and active generation (B9a Design §3, never the desired plan's). A launch held by the resolver (no project, a setup, a rebind waiting for approval) launches nothing; one whose CLI starts after that binding stopped being the active one, or while a rebind waits, is closed unheard and refused, releasing what it claimed. A running session is reused only while its binding is the active one, and replaced once another is installed (an approved transfer); while its directory is held it is kept, with its claim and events (a start asking for another connection then is refused before it is ended), and nothing invalidates it on a desired-plan change alone. Work dispatched to it (`send`, a conversation rewind) is refused while its directory is held or once its binding is stale, sending nothing and ending nothing; `availability(canvas, agent)` says the same without failing, for whatever dispatches to hold rather than fail. createCore composes WorkspaceDirectories for both hosts. Directory availability changes wake pending sends without closing a still-active session; the renderer bridge carries only readiness and words, never a path.

What checks a session before mutating its tree reads it here: `binding(canvas, agent)`, the binding its session launched in (none while none runs), `running`, `conversationOf` (the conversation it resumes), and `closing`, whether a session of it that was closed hasn't finished closing (the bounded close stopped waiting, so its CLI may still run and write: evidence of an uncertain writer, until it has actually closed); `bindings`, including the checkout of an unfinished close, and `canvases` and `agents`, the native sessions and unfinished closes the gate must protect even before a core runtime opens their canvas, and `writing`, their received turn/background activity (updated synchronously on native events, never a second engine); `writingIn(canvas, workspace)`, the agents whose sessions run in a workspace's checkout or were closed there without finishing (what a workspace's cleanup waits for: B9a C7); and `generationOf`, the generation of the agent's running session, which its prompts' Needs ids carry (an earlier session's prompt of a reused native id is another: B11 C7). While the workspace mutation gate restores a tree it works in (`src/main/core/workspaces/gate.ts`), its `send` (its own restore's too) and its launch (another agent's restore, on any canvas) wait for that to finish, failing nothing, and `availability` says `mutating`.

A bounded execFile helper closes its pipes and reports immediately on timeout or excess output while the owner finishes group cleanup before the core exits. A capped Git read instead waits for that cleanup before answering or failing.

## `src/main/sessions/terminal/`

The terminal toolkit, imported by adapters; it imports no adapter and names no CLI. session.ts runs any CLI in a node-pty pseudo-terminal (pty.ts), its turns and views, typing and pasted drops, what it asks, and bounded shutdown. cli.ts is TerminalCli, the adapter's instructions for running and reading it. screen.ts keeps a headless xterm.js copy (snapshots, buffer access, window title, cursor and Program Status reports); status.ts is OSC 7501 Program Status, the vocabulary for states. hooks.ts runs the local server for JSON hook events, with HOOK_TOKEN and HookServer/HookRegistration/HookEvent; drop.ts quotes pasted paths; typing.ts keeps ENTER, and first-run.ts the FirstRunTurn interface.

## Connected sessions

The first live GUI transcript subscription ensures its session is open through SessionControl, as `session.open` does, without another operation in the gesture. Viewing is a lease, never ownership: the last view disappearing leaves the session running. Window/topic leases defer release across StrictMode’s rehearsal and coalesce concurrent opens. A refused launch is shown in the session while its topic remains subscribed. Viewed sessions reopen once their connection and active workspace binding are ready after a change; a pending rebind retains the old session and claim. A project-folder transfer finishes closing the old sessions before the view opens in the new folder. Tokens and internal topic followers never open a session by reading it.

Resume lists and rewinds use the agent's current adapter's conversation provider. A list is its
caller's (`callerKey`: the window that asked, `window:<id>`, an agent's session or an outside token,
never its party), bound to its canvas, agent, adapter and the working-directory binding it was
listed in (folder, workspace and active generation), for ten minutes; the caller's next list
replaces it, and at most eight callers' lists are kept per agent. A list shows at most 100, the most
recent, titles and branch labels each one line of at most 200 UTF-16 units, the whole list within MAX_PAGE_BYTES, and admits only what it shows. A resume is
admitted only from the caller's own current list, never another caller's, and checked again just
before the agent's session is closed (`ConversationRefusal`: `not-found` for one not listed, in
that binding, in time; `conflict` for one another agent's session holds, refused before anything
closes). These are the registered `session.conversations` (O: you and outside tokens, never an
agent, a read) and `session.resumeConversation` (W, agent scope, `self: 'refused'`) over
SessionControl: the listing is refused for an adapter whose catalog `list` is false and its ids
parsed again as `ConversationId` (R42) before they are shown; a token names a conversation only as
the reach resolver finds it in its own last list of that agent (`SessionManager.listedTo`), which
the core checks again in the agent's lane. A token's list is first filtered to successful native conversation claims recorded by this core for that exact canvas, agent, adapter and checkout; another agent's starts and the person's other CLI conversations are never admitted. This start history outlives Fresh and session closure, is cleared on a project change, and is process state: after a core restart, opening the remembered session records its claim again. Your GUI list still shows everything the adapter lists within the display bounds; a resume needs the session idle with nothing waiting to
go and its workspace launchable, then reopens it once (`SessionStore.resumed`). `session.options`
(R, a read) reads an adapter's models and the user's defaults in the named agent's workspace now,
or the canvas's project folder, never a cached canvas-wide answer; an options result over MAX_PAGE_BYTES is refused, without clipping native setting identifiers. Claude Code lists and forks
its kept conversations through the Agent SDK; Codex lists its project's threads through a private
App Server's `thread/list`, without starting a thread. A connection without rewind refuses it.

Malformed native conversation ids in list results are skipped, preserving the valid conversations,
their order and the agent's current marker. The manager and each adapter's reader warn once per
listing with the number skipped, never the invalid ids themselves. Only the valid ids enter the
manager's resume allowlist; an explicit resume or rewind still rejects an invalid id before
closing a session, reading a transcript or forking it. A provider's actual listing failure still
rejects the request.

Native conversation ids meet R42a at every boundary: 1–128 ASCII letters, digits, `.`, `_`,
`:`, or `-`, with no leading `-`, then the adapter's `isConversationId` (UUIDs for both shipped
adapters). IPC checks the minimum; the manager checks the agent's adapter, including list and
fork results and remembered ids. Connectors check resumes before finding/spawning a CLI, and
validate native replies before remembering them. Saved conversation rows with invalid ids are
refused with a diagnostic; an unknown adapter's safe ids are preserved. Canvas memory always
checks the platform minimum; the core supplies the registry's stricter check, so storage and
its test helpers do not load connector runtimes or assets. Terminal hooks refuse
an invalid id with a session warning, once per invalid id in that session, while still
reporting the rest of every event that carries it. Native ids
live in canvas memory, not the run checkpoint. Agent and prompt ids that never enter argv or
native conversation paths keep their own bounds.

**Conversation claims** (`src/main/sessions/claims.ts`, B11 Design §7): the core keeps one
process-wide map of live claims (composed in createCore), keyed by adapter and native id alone,
never a folder, each held by one session generation of a canvas's agent. A launch claims its
remembered conversation at once, nothing awaited between the check and the claim; held by another
agent's session, it starts a new conversation instead, with one warning notice naming the holder
(quoted and escaped as a name) if it is on the same canvas, never one on another (only "an agent on
another canvas": a token granted this canvas alone reads its transcripts), and the holder's saved
conversation is never cleared. An explicit resume is refused, before the caller's session closes,
while another agent's session holds that id; admitted, it is reserved for the agent's next session,
as a rewind's copy is, replacing any conversation of that adapter it reserved for it before. A
native id a session reports later (`remember`, as after /clear or a Terminal's /resume) is claimed
before it is remembered, the session's claim moving off the one it ran before; one another agent's
session holds is a conflict: the reporting session is stopped, with an error notice saying why, and
the id is never remembered. A claim is released only by its own generation, on a failed launch, or
once its session has actually closed (however long after the bounded close stopped waiting), never a
successor's; an agent's later session takes over its earlier one's claim or reservation. A restart
(`restarting`, session.restart's) reserves the conversation it runs for its next session before
closing it, nothing awaited between reading and reserving it, so no other agent's launch takes it
meanwhile, and refuses (before closing anything) while another agent's session holds it; once
closed, a close that didn't finish refuses to start over it, the reservation kept for its next
start. Fresh, a project change and a canvas's sessions ending let its reservations go. Claims are
live state, never kept, and protect nothing outside this core: another profile's core, or a CLI run
outside corrallm, may open the same conversation.

The core watches each live turn for silence (`core/stalled.ts`, in the app and under `serve`): every session event counts as activity, including text, thinking, tools/progress, status and usage. The Agent SDK also counts every incoming SDK message, the App Server its incoming notifications and requests (not another thread's notifications), and both Terminals their hooks, usage readings, changes in the progress a status-line post carries (`StatusLineReading.progress`, which its TerminalCli reads: for Claude Code its cost, API time, lines changed and tokens, never its clock or limits) and changes in parsed Program Status or window-title reports, even without an attached terminal view. Repeated identical reports, a status line posted again on a timer with the same progress (Claude Code's `refreshInterval`, a limit's reset, its prompt cache expiring) and raw pty output do not count: a spinner, elapsed timer or timed status line cannot keep a dropped connection looking alive. Parsed changes include native progress, task state and status messages; corrallm does not guess activity from adapter-specific spinner text. While the last status is waiting, a native prompt is open, or the Terminal's asking list is nonempty, the silence clock is suspended and any stalled Need clears. Permission, question, plan and elicitation events open that wait; withdrawn events remove their prompt ids, and a working status clears native prompt waits (SDK answers do not emit withdrawn). Terminal asks keep their own whole-list lifetime. Leaving the wait starts a fresh twenty minutes, and wake/network changes grant no shorter deadline to a waiting turn. Twenty minutes without activity adds one warning notice to the agent's session, naming its CLI from the catalog, and one corrallm notice to each party whose message is in that turn. The sender notice keeps a `stalled:<message>` told key across restarts and wakes `wait`; coalesced messages from one sender get one notice. It never fails or interrupts a turn automatically. Needs lists `stalled/<agent>` (the agent id escaped), a user-only prompt with Interrupt and Interrupt and resend answered by `reply`. Activity, the turn ending, or the session closing clears it; a later silence can show it again, without repeating that turn's notices. A new turn has its own warning. Main forwards Electron powerMonitor's `resume` over CoreLink; headless, the watchdog's 15-second timer detects a wall-clock gap over a minute. Old turns then get two minutes of fresh silence before warning, without counting time asleep; fresh activity restores the normal twenty-minute deadline. On every tick, in either host, it also reads `os.networkInterfaces()` locally: each non-internal interface's IPv4 addresses and IPv6 /64 prefixes, excluding `fe80::/10` link-local addresses. It skips the container and VM bridges that Docker, Podman, CNI, libvirt and macOS vmnet make and remove as agents start and stop them (interfaces named `docker*`, `br-*`, `veth*`, `virbr*`, `cni*`, `podman*` or `bridge*`; a host bridge such as `br0` still counts, since it can carry the host's own address). The identity includes interface names, normalizes IPv6 notation, and ignores order, duplicate addresses, and temporary IPv6 suffix rotation. The first successful reading is only a baseline. Only a loss is a change: an address or prefix gone or changed, or an interface gone or renamed. A new interface or address (a VPN connecting, say) can't break a connection made before it and is no change; if it later goes, that is. A change gives old silent turns the same two-minute grace, worded "2 minutes after the network changed". The first changed sample is the observed change time (at most one 15-second tick after the OS snapshot changes); subsequent activity clears grace and restores the normal deadline. Repeated wake or network reports never extend an existing grace period. A failed reader preserves the last good identity and logs once per core lifetime, without logging the addresses. The watchdog's clock/timers (`CoreConfig.watchdog`) and interface reader (`CoreConfig.watchdogNetwork`) are injectable; its timer is unref'd, and close cancels it. Restored history starts no watchdog: interrupted turns become unresolved as usual, never silently retried.

The core’s CLIs belong to its profile (`core/children.ts`, B8 Design §6): before opening any canvas it reads `<profile>/sessions/pids.json`, verifies each stale leader’s OS start identity and private process group, and sends that group SIGTERM, then SIGKILL to the whole group after a three-second grace if it still runs. A missing or reused pid is never signalled. Failed cleanup never prevents startup: inspection or signalling failures, or a group surviving SIGKILL, warn with the pid and reason and keep its record for the next start, including across new children’s spawns and exits. Reading or updating the registry failing also warns. An unreadable, malformed or unknown-version registry is renamed to `pids.rejected-<uuid>.json` and the folder synced before any new registry replaces it; the unread bytes stay available for inspection. If preserving it fails, startup continues but CLI spawns and registry replacement are refused. Each new pty and stdio CLI, the Agent SDK’s custom-spawned process included, is recorded atomically and forced to disk before its spawner returns, and removed on exit. As the core closes, after its sessions, it ends every CLI still running (a helper a read in flight started, such as a settings read's app server or the login shell) as it ends a timed-out helper's group, and starts none after, so none outlives it. `sessions/process.ts` carries the profile’s owner through asynchronous operations, channels and engine backends with AsyncLocalStorage, so helper app servers, auth/version/help commands, the login shell and git are covered too; tests and self-test calls outside a composed core have no profile owner. Stdio processes use `detached` on POSIX; node-pty creates its session and group. On POSIX a new CLI must lead its own process group before it is recorded, or it is refused and killed. On Linux node-pty’s `forkpty` returns before its child calls setsid, so a child still in the core’s own group is re-read every millisecond for up to a second, blocking (the spawn hook is synchronous); under CPU contention that has taken over 100 ms. One that leaves the core’s group for any group but its own, or is still in the core’s after that, is refused. One that exits, or whose pid starts a different process, meanwhile is treated as having exited before its identity could be read: it is neither recorded nor signalled. Windows keeps `useConptyDll: true`, records children and forgets exits, but creates no detached console and does no stale-group signalling: Windows process identity and group cleanup remain deferred until before launch. The registry holds only pid and start identity, never arguments, environment or tokens. On Linux that identity is `/proc/<pid>/stat` field 22 (start ticks since boot, kept as an exact decimal string) plus `/proc/sys/kernel/random/boot_id`; the private group comes from the same stat record. Wall-clock steps cannot make an owned CLI look reused. macOS keeps `ps`’s `lstart`; Windows keeps its existing timestamp without stale-group signalling.

## Agent settings

What an agent runs with by an adapter (`AgentSettings` in `src/shared/session.ts`: its model, reasoning effort and the mode its sessions start in, each only if set) is the agent's, in its node's data (`settings`, by adapter: `{ 'claude-code'?, codex? }`, so switching between Claude and Codex loses neither), saved in its `.corral` file (file.ts checks it: a model by `MODEL_PATTERN`, which never starts with a dash, an effort by `EFFORT_PATTERN`, a mode the catalog lists for that adapter with `asks: true` that its connection names in `startModes` (for its own adapter, the connection it runs; for another, any of that adapter's), and nothing else; an adapter the catalog lacks has its settings checked for shape only) and copied to an agent spawned from it. Switching an agent's connection or adapter drops a saved mode the connection it then runs does not start in (shared/model/agent-ops.ts), so what the app saves always reads back. Both of an adapter's connections start with them (`session:start`, which refuses a mode outside the connection's `startModes`); the core keeps none of its own. They are set in the shared Agent settings sheet (`AgentSettings.tsx`), opened from the session-header summary, the summary in All queues, or Settings… in an agent's context menu. It contains Name, CLI/connection, Role, and native startup Model/Effort/Mode. Claude and Codex default to real Terminal connections. An adapter or connection this build lacks says Not connected; missing CLIs fail visibly. No agent simulation or fabricated replies remain. The quick agent chip and a structured session's live composer controls remain shortcuts.

- **Role** is CLI-independent standing instruction text (`data.role`, top-level `role` on a file's agent node), separate from the per-CLI model/effort/mode settings. `shared/role.ts` trims it, removes blank values, rejects non-text/control characters and caps it at 16,000 characters. Templates discover/fill role blanks; changing CLI, inserting a template, and spawning an agent preserve it. A role never becomes a user prompt or Message row.
- **Changes and native conversations.** Editing the role or startup choices saves them without interrupting the session. `Session.running` captures what it started with. Main remembers a SHA-256 role fingerprint (or `none`), never its text, beside each CLI's conversation ID; it is forgotten with that ID. A resume with no fingerprint counts as a changed role once. For Claude, the sheet offers an idle-only restart that resumes the same native conversation; the SDK preserves its claude_code preset and appends the role, while Terminal uses --append-system-prompt. Both disable saved system-prompt snapshots only when the resumed role changed or was removed. Terminal reads --help only for a role, prepared workspace context, an effort or a changed role.
- **Codex limitation.** Current Codex 0.162.1 retains plain developer instructions in resumed history (upstream issue #19045), so a same-ID restart cannot guarantee a changed role. The sheet explains this and offers an explicit idle-only new conversation. `session:fresh` closes the agent, durably clears only the selected CLI's remembered ID and role fingerprint, then opens its current settings; the old native conversation remains available in the CLI's history. Ordinary restart keeps exact-ID resume. Both connections read effective native developer_instructions only for a current role or prepared workspace context (appended on every launch), or a changed role (restoring native instructions when removed).
- **Startup choices.** Model and Effort use native options/defaults. Claude Terminal's Mode is limited to --permission-mode values, Codex Terminal to Read only (Default uses its config, Plan is chosen in the terminal). Structured connections offer their supported modes. Modes that never ask are chosen live, not persisted as startup settings. The structured composer's model/effort/mode controls still change its live session and the saved settings; bypassing permissions or Full access unsets the saved mode.
- **Fallback chain** (B11a): the agent's `fallback` (its next backends, yours alone through `agent.setFallback`: see [Usage](usage.md)). When corrallm switches the agent along it, its session ends at a turn's end, never in one: the engine's sessions hold whatever would start a turn or a session from the moment it has nothing going (`SessionStore.switching`: what is sent meanwhile waits in its queue and goes, in order, once the switch settles, on the connection the agent runs then; a restart or a new conversation asked meanwhile waits for it too), its session is closed, a close the manager stopped waiting for is waited out (`SessionManager.closed`, which resolves once no close of the agent's is unfinished), and the target adapter's remembered conversation is forgotten (`fresh`), so the target starts a fresh conversation through the normal launch, with the step's model and effort written into that adapter's settings, else the agent's saved ones, else the CLI's. Nothing native crosses: no conversation id, mode, approval, permission rule, MCP login, setting or credential.
- **Handoff** (B11a C7): the agent's `handoff` settings (yours alone through `agent.setHandoff`). The core reads each session's `usage` reports' `context` (`SessionUsage.context`, in whole tokens on its connection's own basis, as its adapter reports it) and its turns' ends, as the engine reduces them (`SessionStore.observe`): no session is asked anything mid-turn, and none whose connection's descriptor says it reports no context (`context: false`, every connection until X3's recordings prove one) or whose session reported none. Its request is a message from corrallm, delivered at a turn's end like any other, and its reply is the agent's own text; the next backend after a switch starts a fresh conversation whose first automated send carries it (see [Usage](usage.md)).

What a CLI's agents can be set to, and the user's own defaults, come from `session.options` (one CLI; the core reads in the named agent's workspace now, else the canvas's project folder, else the home folder, with the agents' environment, never changing anything; no window caches it):

- Claude Code (`claude-settings.ts`): no prompt, no session, and never the Agent SDK on a subscription, so no model list from the CLI. The models are the aliases `claude --model` takes (opus, fable, sonnet, haiku: Claude Code's descriptions from 2.1.296's list), only those in the user's `availableModels` where set, plus their own model if it is none of them, each with Claude Code's effort levels. The defaults are read as Claude Code reads its settings (project local, project, user in CLAUDE_CONFIG_DIR or ~/.claude: `model`, `effortLevel`, `permissions.defaultMode`), with ANTHROPIC_MODEL and CLAUDE_CODE_EFFORT_LEVEL (environment, or a settings file's `env`) over them; managed settings are not read.
- Codex (`codex/options.ts`): a private `codex app-server` (the folder trusted for it alone, as a Terminal session trusts it) asked only `model/list` and `config/read` (no thread, no model asked, so no usage and no consent on a ChatGPT plan), then closed: Codex's models with their efforts and default effort, and the config's model (else model/list's `isDefault`), `model_reasoning_effort` and the preset its sandbox and approvals make (recorded in `app-server/fixtures/options.jsonl`).

## Shared Terminal sessions and views

Both Terminal adapters register SessionStart, but it does not reliably report permissions before a prompt. Codex 0.160.1 fires it with the first turn; Claude Code 2.1.310 fires it at startup without `permission_mode`, even with an explicit start mode. SessionManager captures the adapter's trusted default immediately before launching a Terminal with no explicit mode, in that agent's resolved cwd and environment; this also covers sessions opened directly by a window. The shared session carries it in `started.asks`, or reports the explicit start mode, until a recognized native hook reports a mode. A native report received during launch wins over the baseline. App Server and Agent SDK sessions still report their own permissions; they never inherit this Terminal fallback.

An unreported Terminal with hooks on waits silently for at most 30 seconds: no unasking notice or Need during startup. Delivery schedules that deadline, scoped to the particular running session. A native report releases it or establishes an unasking hold at once; closing, replacing or restarting the session discards its old deadline. With hooks off, or after the deadline without a report, it becomes unknown-running, with an actionable notice and Need saying to check the terminal, switch to an asking mode or enable its hooks. A failed default read never grants permission, and hooks disabled by user or managed settings are still respected. The initial default is not a live settings poll: a switch made inside a terminal reaches delivery only when a hook reports it.

Before a Terminal CLI starts, its shared session writes a 0600 `hook-header` in its private temporary folder. Its children inherit only the non-secret `CORRALLM_HOOK_HEADER` path; `CORRALLM_HOOK_TOKEN` is stripped even if inherited from the caller. Both adapters' command hooks pass that path to curl's header-file option, never expanding a secret into argv. The file lives until the session closes, including native conversation restarts within it. Claude Code's native HTTP hooks keep literal headers in its 0600 settings file; its header-variable allowlist does not disable these hooks because they interpolate no variables.

- A Terminal session (`sessions/terminal/session.ts`) runs whatever CLI its TerminalCli describes (`cli.ts`): how to start and resume it (in the user's own default mode unless given one; unknown until its reports say), what its screen reports (Program Status, or its window title), what its hooks mean (`TerminalSignal`s: its session id, prompts it took, its reply, a failure, its state when its screen does not say, its mode and model, what it is about to ask the user, what its notifications say it needs), how to read its prompt's draft and type into it, and its words (`words`: what it waits on, by its report's kind). Turns, waiting on the user, what it asks listed outside its terminal (`asking`), typing the queue's messages once the prompt is ready, files dropped on it pasted in, views and closing are the same for every CLI. A turn its CLI ends with an error fails with the reason its hooks gave; where they gave none and its CLI reads its account's usage apart from its terminal (`readUsage`), the session reads it before the turn ends, and a reading that says the account is used up (`TerminalUsage.usedUp`) makes it `usage-limit` until then (else `error`). While that read is pending, later session events wait behind the end, keeping each turn's identity. If the next turn ends or the session closes first, the pending turn ends as `error` and those events are released in order; a late read never emits a second end. A CLI that picks its own session ids (`newId()` is null) is remembered once its hooks tell it. A CLI whose reports leave out some dialogs (`waitsForPrompt`) is typed into only while its prompt is drawn, one that holds a message while it gets ready (`holds()`) is waited for rather than given up on, and one that reads keys late (`waitsForEcho`: Claude Code) gets Enter once its prompt shows the end of what was typed (at most `TerminalTiming.echo`).

A Terminal session's `user` event acknowledges submission, not enqueueing: corrallm emits it after typing the message and sending Enter. Messages held at startup, folder trust or a user's draft have no receipt, even though their turn already reports waiting or working. Cancelling them ends their turn without telling delivery, or withdrawal notes, that the CLI received them. Hook notices use the same end-ordering buffer as the other session events.

- The view, for every Terminal agent: files dropped on it are pasted as their paths (the preload reads them from the files with `webUtils.getPathForFile`; `terminal.drop` checks them; the core pastes those that exist, quoted for a shell, `drop.ts`, bracketed when the CLI asked, so Claude Code takes an image's path as the image). On a Mac, Option works as Meta only on a US-like keyboard layout (`navigator.keyboard.getLayoutMap`; elsewhere Option types characters, as @ on a German layout), unless the user chose otherwise with the switch in the bar (kept in localStorage). When the screen is reset (a snapshot, as for a new CLI process), the view fits it and draws it afresh (`Screen.redraw`). Links Cmd-clicked in it go to main (`link.open`; a file:// link to `link.reveal`).

- Terminal views: `terminal.attach` asks the core to show an agent's terminal; the core sends the port's `event('terminal', [agent, TerminalEvent])`, heard through the typed client's `terminalOnEvent` (`src/shared/terminal.ts`): the screen as it is (a snapshot of its headless copy, now or once the session starts, and again for each new process), then the CLI's output, then its exit. The view (`link.ts`) writes the snapshot from scratch, drops what its terminal answers while doing so, then passes keystrokes (`terminal.input`) and its size (`terminal.resize`) on. The pty lives in the core: closing the view (`terminal.detach`) leaves the session running. While no view is shown, the core's copy answers the terminal queries a pty needs answered (DA1, which Windows' ConPTY waits on).

A stalled turn’s explicit Interrupt and resend decision is consumed once for that live turn, before the interrupt is sent. Its cancelled end is the trigger; a completed turn, a session that closed, a withdrawn party message, or another turn’s ids never authorize a resend. The reply returns once the interrupt is admitted (`SessionManager.interruptIf`), never waiting in its canvas's lane for the CLI's answer, which a wedged CLI may never give: an interrupt the CLI refuses, or hasn't answered within `STALLED_INTERRUPT_MS` (30 seconds, including a pending session launch), arms no resend and gives the Need back, answerable, unless the turn was active since. The manager rechecks the captured turn and session after awaiting launch outside the operation’s lane; an expired request never interrupts a late launch or a newer turn.

The core keeps each live outgoing message until its turn ends, including original text and live images. Provenance and task ownership belong to durable mailbox rows, not connector-facing UserMessage fields. Native prompt echoes fill in text for submissions made directly in a terminal. A party's resend is a new durable message from that party to the same agent, with origin `resend` and `resendOf` naming the original. It waits on normal delivery holds. Manual submissions get UUID records linked to native ids by `delivery.as`. Neither message rows nor checkpoints persist image bytes: a restored resend without them becomes unresolved and asks you to paste them again. A terminal echo supplies no image bytes.

A run retries through its held problem's Retry decision. Stalled Interrupt and resend reserves the attempts from durable messages and, on that native turn's cancelled end, calls `Engines.retryAttempt` (see [Engine](engine.md)). No event goes to a window or its engine. Pauses, usage rules, permission modes and exhaustion hold retries; a changed task, Kill, closed session or unrelated end drops the request. The old attempt stays cancelled and the new attempt's `retryOf` links to it in run JSON. A crash leaves ordinary unresolved work; startup never infers a retry decision.

The window operation surface exposes the C6 controls and typed native-prompt replies through the same services as tokens. setMode and continueIn remain yours alone (D9); a token never lists or calls them. Their results carry changed runtime topic revisions, plus a canvas revision when saved settings changed. Session components keep their existing plumbing until X13.

The app and serve use the same core-owned sessions and run. A window reconnects to their topics; closing, reloading or changing its canvas does not stop or restore a session. AgentView takes its summary and entries together from the per-session topic, using the canvas summary only before that snapshot arrives. Opening or switching an agent sends no session.open: its transcript subscription supplies the viewing lease. An explicit Try again still uses session.open. The foreground connection picker and Continue use session.continueIn; its pending text and live images remain exact, without a second open or send. Composer mode/model/effort, interrupt, task Stop, restart, new conversation, rewind and native prompt answers use their registered composites/typed reply. Saved startup settings and a settings-sheet connection edit remain document operations. Terminal attachment and screen bytes keep their existing stream.

Your composer's message is the registered `message` (B11 §3, §16.6): the core admits it as your manual message, up to MAX_MESSAGE_LENGTH characters with the live images its recipient's connection takes (its catalog `images`), into the same durable mailbox a token's goes through; a token's is held to 16 KiB of text by the `human-text` rule. /mcp is `session.mcpServers` (R, a read) and `session.changeMcpServer` (L, agent scope, `self: 'refused'`) over SessionControl: refused where the agent's connection declares no `panels.mcp` (or not that change), acting only on a server its running session reports now (else not-found), never a command or a URL; a change says so in the agent's transcript (an info notice naming who, if not you) and answers with every server as it is then, so the panel refreshes without a second operation. The `corral-mcp` argument rule refuses every token's change to corrallm's own fixed entries (`corrallm-server` and the reserved agent entry `corrallm`), for every change including reconnect; your GUI may change them. Other servers retain L and the self refusal. Both answer at most `MAX_MCP_SERVERS`, names within `MAX_MCP_NAME_LENGTH` and errors cut to `MAX_MCP_ERROR_LENGTH`. A command the agent view answers itself in a panel (one its connection's catalog `panels` names: /resume, /mcp, /rewind), typed in your composer, is `session.viewCommand` (yours alone, the `view` deviation, agent scope): `Delivery.record` keeps it as your manual message, settled at once (`replied`, its turn completed) and never delivered, so no CLI turn; anything else is invalid. The panel then reads, or acts, through its own operations (a view's reads are no gesture). The session summary (session.get, `sessions:<c>`, the per-session topic's) also carries, for you alone (session.get's `varies`, `summaryFor`), the CLI's `suggestion` (whole, within `MAX_SUGGESTION_BYTES`; a longer one is left out, never cut) and its terminal's `asking` (`askingView`: the newest `MAX_TERMINAL_ASKS` whose ids a Need can name, each text cut to its bound and marked `cut`, the newest that fit `MAX_ASKING_BYTES` together, so the summary fits its begin frame); a token sees how many prompts there are and Needs' own words. Pasted images' bytes reach no topic: a window previews what it sent itself (see [Renderer](renderer.md)). No production renderer imports SessionBackend or calls a session channel.

## Prepared workspace-context adapter seam

`SessionSpec.workspaceContext` is separate from the saved role and is not a queue item or user message. `src/main/sessions/instructions.ts` bounds the supplied UTF-8 context to 8 KiB (rejecting overlong text rather than clipping a ref), then combines it with the role for the native startup append seam. Role-only launches keep their exact previous text. All four connectors consume it through their existing native instruction paths; native defaults, approval policies, modes and message provenance remain unchanged. Claude Terminal probes prompt options for context as it does for a role; both Codex connections read effective native developer instructions before appending context.

This is an uncomposed consumer, not a context authorizer. SessionManager supplies no workspace context until C provides the reached-member/ref/base projection and freshness/conversation-binding policy. Directory admission already checks active workspace generations, but this change does not invent a context fingerprint, native snapshot reset or new-conversation decision. In particular the existing Codex retained-instruction limitation cannot be solved by appending newer refs to a resumed history. See [the C gaps](workspaces.md#prepared-workspace-context-consumer-c-gaps).

## Context report seam

`SessionUsage.context` carries nullable `{ used, window, compactAt }` in whole tokens on the connection's own meter basis. Adapters calculate it; the Terminal toolkit only forwards and deduplicates its `TerminalCli`'s status-line or title/hook reading. It forwards context before a title or hook ends a turn and clears a prior reading when the adapter can no longer say. Legacy `contextTokens` and `contextWindow` accompany the report. The generic fake emits a deterministic context report before each turn ends. `context: true` requires sanitized recordings of the real connection and its automatic compaction, not only fake tests (B11a C4): Claude Code's Agent SDK and Terminal, and Codex's App Server declare it, each pinned by its adapter's contract test (`adapter.test.ts`) and replay. Codex's Terminal declares `context: false`: its title percentage is recorded, but production reads no usable-window source for it (see [Codex](codex.md)). Recording a compaction costs little with the CLI's own knobs set for the one run (`CLAUDE_CODE_AUTO_COMPACT_WINDOW=100000`, a per-run `-c model_context_window=50000` and `model_auto_compact_token_limit` for Codex), never the user's files; those knobs are recorded in each fixture's header.

B13 X8 exposes the owner's reply shapes through the registry and carries a form's configured `server` in its nested Needs prompt metadata. The old renderer cards and their SessionHost bridge remain until X10/X11; registering the new shapes does not remove mirrored prompts or change session ownership, native answer validation or the owner's captured handle.

The manager’s final-send hook carries the receiving native session’s connection; a later document choice cannot substitute another connection’s input rules for that send.

## Prepared agent API contracts

`src/main/sessions/connector.ts` prepares `SessionSpec.api?: SessionApi`: an HTTP route with server name `corrallm`, URL and token, or an in-process route with a caller-bound `AgentChannel` and no token. Both carry readonly tool grants (`name`, `destructive`) and outside server names to switch off. The channel lists `ToolListing` values and calls with unchanged arguments and an AbortSignal, returning the same MCP `CallToolResult` as HTTP. These are private connector startup types, never events or renderer data. Every connection's descriptor keeps `api: null`; native entry installation belongs to each adapter (X2), and turning a connection's route on to its recording (C7).

## Session credentials

SessionManager takes the core's session authority as `deps.api` (`issue`, `revoke`; `src/main/core/agent-api.ts`). `#launch` asks `issue` once per generation, after the working directory is admitted and the remembered conversation claimed and before `connector.start`, only while that generation is still the agent's session, and passes what it returns as `SessionSpec.api`; when nothing is issued the spec has no `api` and the session starts without corrallm's server. Where the connection's descriptor declares a route but nothing was issued (the endpoint isn't listening), the transcript says so once, naming no value. The manager revokes the generation's credential beside every end of its prompts (`#gone`, before `prompts.gone`): as its close begins (restart, new conversation, connection or backend switch, deletion, canvas close or switch, project or workspace switch, quit), on a failed start, on a `closed` event, and as an install is found unheard or its start times out, before that session's close runs. A generation whose close began while it launched is never issued one. Rotation is by generation: a restart, new conversation, fallback switch or connection switch gets a new credential even when it resumes the same native conversation; a window's reload, crash or close rotates nothing.

A connector start that fails revokes its generation's credential before awaiting owned-child cleanup; its generation stays unheard while cleanup finishes. The later prompt teardown may revoke it again idempotently.
